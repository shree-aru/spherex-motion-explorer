# Judge demo — ethical, original SPHEREx investigation workbench

This demonstration should be rehearsed **only after live SPHEREx archive retrieval succeeds**. Do not play a synthetic FITS animation or claim a measured displacement without actual evidence.

## First minute — show the required task

Open the web app, not a promotional video. Search for Barnard's Star (or a field with confirmed matched real QR3 observations). Show the coordinate controls, selecting a wide survey and a UTC range. Retrieve observations from NASA/IPAC IRSA. Choose the same detector range for several different UTC dates, add them with **+**, select **Sequence** and play. Pause. Display the exact UTC date and scientific provenance.

## Next 30 seconds — explain trust

Show two compatible exposure records in **A/B Blink** and **B − A**. Explain that science and quality flags were read from FITS, the positions were reprojected onto one celestial grid, and the current difference is only an exploratory residual because PSF and wavelength matching are not yet certified. Never describe a residual as a confirmed new planet.

## Next 30 seconds — what makes SPHEREx special

Change the detector-range filter, explaining the instrument's 102 spectral bands and the spatially varying linear variable filters. Highlight that 102-channel coadds/spectrum extraction remain planned until vetted calibration is implemented. Open the separately labelled Aladin reference atlas and distinguish it from SPHEREx imagery.

## Next 20 seconds — reproducibility and open science

Share the selected coordinates, data release, observations and mode through the view URL. Export a JSON evidence manifest. Show that missing observations cause a clear error rather than invented imagery. State that this is an independent public-benefit application, built with credited public data and open libraries.

## Validation targets (do not promise same results from each)

- Barnard's Star: approximate large proper motion with parallax caveat.
- ε Indi B: nearby binary brown dwarf; confirm correct component centre and field size.
- WISE 0855−0714: epoch-sensitive coordinates and high proper motion; historical coordinates can mislead.
- RW Aurigae: brightness changes from a young variable star (requires photometric matching).
- R Draconis: Mira variable, valuable for multi-epoch variability, not fast sky motion.

**Competitive message:** We are turning a complex public space archive into an accessible, repeatable observation workflow. Our first priority is a trustworthy instrument that shows what the data prove—and what they do not.
